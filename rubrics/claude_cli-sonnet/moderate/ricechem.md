## ricechem/Q1 (8 marks)

**Question:** When studying the emission sources within the Milky Way, a satellite detected interplanetary clouds containing silicon atoms that have lost five electrons.
b) The ionization energies corresponding to the removal of the third, fourth, and fifth electrons in silicon are 3231, 4356, and 16091 kJ/mol, respectively. 
Using core charge calculations and your understanding of Coulomb's Law, briefly explain 1) why the removal of each additional electron requires more energy than the removal of the previous one, and 2) the relative magnitude of the values observed.
This question can be answered reasonably in around 150 words or fewer.

**Model answer:** A full-mark answer contains all of the following:
- correctly cites decreased electron electron repulsion (1 marks)
- relates decreased electron electron repulsion to decreased potential energy (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized feel same core charge (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized from n=3 shell and have same radius (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and feels higher core charge (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and has smaller radius (1 marks)
- correctly explains relationship of potential energy to ionization energy (2 marks)

_Marking stance: moderate. Full marks need each specific claim stated with its cause-and-effect link: for example "same core charge" (not "similar attraction"), and potential energy linked to ionization energy. Part marks go to half-stated ideas, such as only the core charge or only the radius. A bare restatement of the data or a general definition of ionization energy earns nothing, and a wrong statement loses only the marks of its own point._

- **P1** (2): States that removing each electron decreases electron–electron repulsion (fewer electrons remain), and links this to a decrease in potential energy of the remaining electrons (so the remaining electrons are held more tightly / more energy needed for the next removal). _Partial: 1 mark if decreased electron–electron repulsion is stated but not linked to decreased potential energy._
- **P2** (2): 3rd and 4th electrons are removed from the same n=3 shell, so they feel the same core charge and have the same radius; this is why their ionization energies (3231 and 4356) are similar in magnitude / only modestly different. _Partial: 1 mark if only same core charge or only same shell/radius is stated, or the claim is given without the link to similar values._
- **P3** (2): 5th electron is removed from the n=2 shell (inner shell), so it feels a higher core charge and has a smaller radius than the 3rd/4th electrons, so it is much closer to the nucleus and attracted much more strongly (hence the large jump to 16091). _Partial: 1 mark if only higher core charge or only smaller radius/inner shell is stated._
- **P4** (2): Correctly explains the relationship of potential energy to ionization energy: lower (more negative) potential energy, from greater attraction (Coulomb's law: higher core charge, smaller distance), means more energy is needed to remove the electron, so ionization energy is larger. _Partial: 1 mark if the link between attraction/potential energy and ionization energy is stated only partly or vaguely._
