## ricechem/Q1 (8 marks)

**Question:** When studying the emission sources within the Milky Way, a satellite detected interplanetary clouds containing silicon atoms that have lost five electrons.
b) The ionization energies corresponding to the removal of the third, fourth, and fifth electrons in silicon are 3231, 4356, and 16091 kJ/mol, respectively. 
Using core charge calculations and your understanding of Coulomb's Law, briefly explain 1) why the removal of each additional electron requires more energy than the removal of the previous one, and 2) the relative magnitude of the values observed.
This question can be answered reasonably in around 150 words or fewer.

**Model answer:** A full-mark answer contains all of the following:
- correctly cites decreased electron electron repulsion (1 marks)
- relates decreased electron electron repulsion to decreased potential energy (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized feel same core charge (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized from n=3 shell and have same radius (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and feels higher core charge (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and has smaller radius (1 marks)
- correctly explains relationship of potential energy to ionization energy (2 marks)

_Marking stance: harsh. Full marks need every element stated explicitly with correct terms (core charge, shell/n level, radius, potential energy, electron–electron repulsion) and the cause-and-effect chain spelled out. Part marks only where clearly more than half of a point is stated precisely, as given in partial_credit. Vague, generic or merely implied statements (e.g. "more attraction", "closer to nucleus" without shell or radius) earn nothing, and a wrong statement within a point cancels that point._

- **P1** (1): States explicitly that removing each successive electron leaves fewer electrons, so there is decreased electron–electron repulsion in the remaining ion.
- **P2** (1): Explicitly links the decreased electron–electron repulsion to decreased potential energy (lower potential energy of the remaining electrons/ion), step by step, using the term potential energy.
- **P3** (2): States that the 3rd and 4th electrons removed are both from the n=3 shell, so they feel the same core charge AND have the same radius (same shell), with both claims explicit and correct. _Partial: 1 mark if only one of the two (same core charge, or same n=3 shell/radius) is stated precisely._
- **P4** (2): States that the 5th electron is removed from the n=2 shell (inner shell), so it feels a higher core charge AND has a smaller radius than the 3rd/4th electrons, using the terms core charge and radius. _Partial: 1 mark if only one of the two (higher core charge, or n=2 shell/smaller radius) is stated precisely._
- **P5** (2): Correctly explains, via Coulomb's law, the relationship of potential energy to ionization energy: lower potential energy (stronger attraction, higher core charge/smaller radius) means more energy needed to remove the electron, so ionization energy rises; this accounts for the small increase from 3rd to 4th (3231 to 4356) and the large jump to the 5th (16091). Cause and effect must be spelled out. _Partial: 1 mark if only the general link between potential energy (or attraction) and ionization energy is stated, without applying it to the relative magnitudes of the values._
