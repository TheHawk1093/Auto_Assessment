## ricechem/Q1 (8 marks)

**Question:** When studying the emission sources within the Milky Way, a satellite detected interplanetary clouds containing silicon atoms that have lost five electrons.
b) The ionization energies corresponding to the removal of the third, fourth, and fifth electrons in silicon are 3231, 4356, and 16091 kJ/mol, respectively. 
Using core charge calculations and your understanding of Coulomb's Law, briefly explain 1) why the removal of each additional electron requires more energy than the removal of the previous one, and 2) the relative magnitude of the values observed.
This question can be answered reasonably in around 150 words or fewer.

**Model answer:** A full-mark answer contains all of the following:
- correctly cites decreased electron electron repulsion (1 marks)
- relates decreased electron electron repulsion to decreased potential energy (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized feel same core charge (1 marks)
- 3rd and 4th electrons ionized from n=3 shell and have same radius (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and feels higher core charge (1 marks)
- 5th electron ionized from n=2 shell and has smaller radius (1 marks)
- correctly explains relationship of potential energy to ionization energy (2 marks)

_Marking stance: standard. Full marks need each listed idea; equivalent wording accepted. Core charge numbers need not match exactly if the reasoning (same for 3rd/4th, higher for 5th) is correct. No marks for merely restating that the values increase._

- **P1** (1): States that removing electrons reduces electron–electron repulsion (fewer electrons remaining, so remaining electrons are held more tightly).
- **P2** (1): Links the reduced electron–electron repulsion to lower (more negative) potential energy of the remaining electrons.
- **P3** (1): States that the 3rd and 4th electrons removed feel the same core charge (equal effective nuclear charge, e.g. +4 for the n=3 electrons).
- **P4** (1): States that the 3rd and 4th electrons are both removed from the n=3 shell and so have the same (similar) radius.
- **P5** (1): States that the 5th electron is removed from the n=2 shell (inner shell) and feels a higher core charge (e.g. +10 minus 2 other n=2 electrons... greater than that of n=3 electrons).
- **P6** (1): States that the 5th electron, in the n=2 shell, is at a much smaller radius (closer to the nucleus).
- **P7** (2): Correctly relates potential energy to ionization energy using Coulomb's law (V ∝ −Q₁Q₂/r): lower (more negative) potential energy, from higher core charge and/or smaller radius, means more energy is needed to remove the electron. Use this to explain that IE3 and IE4 are similar-ish (modest increase from reduced repulsion) while IE5 is a much larger jump. _Partial: 1 mark if Coulomb's law relationship to ionization energy is stated correctly but not applied to explain the relative magnitudes (or vice versa)._
