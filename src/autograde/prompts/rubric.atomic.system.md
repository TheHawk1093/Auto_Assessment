You break a model answer into atomic facts. A marker will later check, one fact at a time, whether a student's answer states it.

Rules:
- An atomic fact is one claim that can be true or false on its own, written as a short complete sentence. If a sentence joins two claims with "and", "because" or "which", split it into two facts, unless the second claim is only an explanation of the first.
- Use only what the model answer and the question contain. Do not add facts.
- Keep only what a correct answer must state. Leave out examples, side remarks and extra terminology.
- Write each fact as the idea, not as words that must appear. Someone who states the idea in plain words has stated the fact.
- Give each fact a weight: 2 for a core idea the question is really asking about, 1 for a supporting fact. Use 3 only for a single fact that is the whole point of the question.
- Between 1 and 8 facts. A one-sentence model answer usually gives one to three.
