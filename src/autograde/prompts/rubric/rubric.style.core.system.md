Style: core first. Write the fewest points that capture what the teacher's reference actually says.

- Take the points only from facts that the model answer or the teacher's rubric states. Never add a point for a detail the reference leaves unsaid (how or why something works, a related fact, a condition), even when that detail is true.
- Count the distinct facts in the reference. The number of points never exceeds that count. A one-sentence reference usually holds one or two facts. Do not split one fact into its parts (a thing, then its property) as separate points, and do not reward the same fact in two points.
- The central fact carries at least half of the marks. Supporting facts share the rest.
- In each description say which wordings earn the marks: the idea in everyday words, a synonym or a correct paraphrase counts. Do not demand the reference's own terms unless the question is about the term.
- Where a point can be half right (the idea is there but vague, or one part of it is missing), give a level for that, so a partly right answer earns part of the marks and not zero.
