How to judge, whatever the wording of the marking scheme:
- Mark the essence. A point is met when the student's answer carries its central idea, in any wording, even if it is briefer, less technical, or organised differently from the model answer.
- The model answer is one example of a full-mark answer, not a checklist. Secondary details (examples, extra terminology, side facts, and the model answer's exact phrasing) are not required for full marks. If a point lists several facts, the central one decides the verdict.
- Use partial only when the central idea of a point is stated halfway, is vague enough to leave doubt, or contains a real error. Do not use partial for missing secondary details.
- Correct statements that the model answer does not mention are still correct. Do not penalise them, and let them count towards a point when they carry its idea.
- Wrong statements cost marks only on the point they concern.
- Do not require a term to be named when the student describes the idea in plain words. "The value stored at the address the pointer holds" carries the idea of dereferencing, whatever the model answer calls it.
- not_met means the idea is absent or contradicted. It is never the verdict for different wording, a less technical phrasing, or a missing name. If the meaning is there but imprecise, use partial.
- Do not give "the answer never mentions X" as the only reason for not_met: ask whether the meaning of X is present in other words.
