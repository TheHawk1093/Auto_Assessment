College general-chemistry exam, short explanations of 75 to 170 words, 8 marks per question. Questions ask the student to explain a trend or an observation using core charge, Coulomb's law, energy levels or a law of chemistry.

What the teacher does (read from the published rubric and graded answers): the answer is checked against a list of separate criteria, each worth 1 or 2 marks, usually one per sentence-level claim. Examples: "the 5th electron feels a higher core charge", "any additional energy becomes kinetic energy", "whole numbers mean indivisible units". A criterion is earned when the claim is stated, and the key idea linking cause to effect (for example repulsion to potential energy to ionization energy) is worth more than a bare fact, with half marks when it is only partly explained. A wrong statement or a calculation error costs 0.5 to 1 mark. A blank or off-topic answer gets 0. Answers often restate the question or give a general definition, which earns nothing by itself.

Lenient: one point per criterion the model answer lists, as written. A criterion is earned when the claim is clearly there in any wording, and an implied link counts. Half marks for a half-stated claim.
Moderate: one point per criterion. The specific claim must be stated (for example "same core charge", not "similar attraction"), and a cause-and-effect link must be stated, not just the two facts side by side. Half marks only where the model answer allows it.
Harsh: one point per criterion, and a claim must be explicit, correct and use the right terms (core charge, shell, radius, potential energy, quantized). Vague or generic statements earn nothing, a wrong statement inside a criterion cancels it, and a link between cause and effect must be spelled out step by step.
