Incorrect: You wrote that finite differencing "introduces an extra pole which makes the plant unstable". This is not the mechanism. Differencing does not change the plant model. The encoder reports position in discrete counts, so each reading carries a one-count error. Differencing divides that error by the sample period T, so a small count error becomes a large spurious velocity. At 1 kHz, a 0.01° count produces about 10°/s of false velocity. Revisit how the encoder's quantisation step propagates through (θk − θk−1)/T, and estimate the resulting velocity error at the 1 kHz sample rate.
Incorrect: You wrote that differencing "cannot be implemented digitally because the derivative of a sampled signal is not defined". Broadband sensor or electrical noise on the position signal is therefore amplified most at high frequencies, where the true motion carries little information. Consider the frequency response of a differencing operation and what it does to broadband noise superimposed on the position signal.
Overall, the first thing to improve is how the encoder's quantisation step is magnified when divided by the small sample period, because this is the central reason raw differencing fails.
