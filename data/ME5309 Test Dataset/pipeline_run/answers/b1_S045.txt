Q3(a).
First, numerical differentiation amplifies high-frequency encoder noise and quantisation be-
cause a small position error is divided by the short sample interval. Second, differencing is
sensitive to sampling/timing and can produce a noisy, jittery velocity signal; filtering reduces
noise but adds lag.
In a finite difference, the change in position is divided by the sample interval. Encoder quan-
tization error appears in the difference of two measurements, so it can be magnified by the
division by Ts. The result may jump around even if the actual motion is smooth.

Q4(d).
At steady state, a stable closed loop with integral action drives ξ ̇ =r-θ to zero; therefore r-θ=0
and the tracking error is zero, assuming no saturation or other implementation limits prevent
convergence. A practical failure mode is integrator windup during actuator saturation; the
standard remedy is anti-windup, such as back-calculation or conditional integration.
With integral action, a constant tracking error keeps accumulating in the integral state, so the
controller can adjust its output until the error goes to zero, assuming the closed loop is stable
and the actuator is not constrained. Under saturation the integral state can keep growing; anti-
windup limits or back-calculates that state.
