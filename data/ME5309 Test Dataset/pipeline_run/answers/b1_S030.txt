At rest, θ̇ = θ̈ = 0, so the torque balance is Kp(θd-θss) = mgℓ sin θss. With Kp = 50, θd = π/2 and mgℓ
= 2.4525, solve 50(π/2-θss) = 2.4525 sin θss. This gives θss approximately 1.52181 rad = 87.193°,
so the error is 90°-87.193° = 2.807°; it is not zero.
Rearranging the steady-state equation gives 50(π/2 − θss) = 2 .4525 sin θss. I used radians in the
equation. Solving numerically gives θss ≈ 1.522 rad ≈ 87.19◦, so the gap from 90◦ is about 2.81◦.
Q2(d).
With gravity compensation, the gravity term cancels the plant’s mgℓ sin θ term. At rest, Kp(θd-
θss)=0, so θss=θd and the steady-state error is zero. The linearised gravity stiffness term is also
cancelled, so the closed-loop poles generally change; the stiffness contribution mgℓ cos θd is
removed.
The compensation term cancels the nominal gravity torque in the plant model. At steady state
the proportional term must then be zero, which requires θd − θss = 0 . This does not mean
the transient response is unchanged: removing the gravity derivative changes the linearized
stiffness.
