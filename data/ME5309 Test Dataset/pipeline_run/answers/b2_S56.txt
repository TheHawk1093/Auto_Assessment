Q2(c). At rest θ˙ = θ¨ = 0, so the PD torque must balance gravity: Kp(θd − θss) = mgℓ sin θss.
If θss = θd = 90° the left side is 0 but the right side is mgℓ sin 90° = 2.4525 ≠ 0: contradiction. So
the joint cannot rest at θd; the gravity torqe needs a non-zero error to produce the holding torque.
Solve 50(π/2 − θss) = 2.4525 sin θss numerically: θss ≈ 1.5218 rad = 87.19°. Error ≈ 90° − 87.19°
= 2.81° (joint sags below θd).
Q2(d). With compensation, at rest: Kp(θd − θss) + mgℓ sin θss = mgℓ sin θss ⇒ θss = θd: steady-state
error = 0.
Closed loop: Jθ¨ + (b + Kd)θ˙ + Kp(θ − θd) = 0; gravity cancels. Without compensation the
linearised stiffness is Kp + mgℓ cos θd, and at θd = 90° cos θd = 0, so the stiffness is Kp either way.
Poles unchanged (specific to 90°; at other angles they would shift).

size)/T; at low speed the estimate is a coarse staircase of zeros and spikes.

Q3(d). Advantage of the obsever: it uses the plant model and the applied input u, so its velocity
estimat has (almost) no phase lag; the 20 Hz filter on differenced data always delays by about 1/(2π
fc) ≈ 8 ms. It can also reconstruct states that cannot be obtained by differencing (e.g. disturbance,
when augmented).
Filter is safer when the model is uncertain or changes (unknown payload, friction, contact, acuator
saturation): the observer then gives a biased or poorly-behaved estimate while the model-free fliter
still follows the measured data.
