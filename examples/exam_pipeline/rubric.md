# Marking rubric

## Question 1 (5 marks)
1 mark: stress defined as force (or internal resisting force) per unit area, sigma = F/A.
1 mark: strain defined as change in length per unit original length, and noted as dimensionless.
1 mark: the difference made clear (stress is the cause or loading, strain is the resulting deformation).
1 mark: Hooke's law stated: stress proportional to strain within the limit of proportionality or elastic limit.
1 mark: sigma = E epsilon, with E as Young's modulus, a constant of the material.

## Question 2 (5 marks)
1 mark: area of a circle from the diameter, A = pi d^2 / 4 (or pi r^2 with r = 10 mm).
1 mark: area calculated correctly, 314.16 mm^2 (or 3.14 x 10^-4 m^2).
1 mark: load converted to newtons, 50 kN = 50 000 N, with consistent units.
1 mark: stress = F / A.
1 mark: correct answer, 159 MPa (accept 159 to 160), with the unit stated.
A number with no working earns no marks.

## Question 3 (5 marks)
(a) 2 marks: 1 mark for the formula pi d^2 / 4, 1 mark for the value 113.1 mm^2.
(b) 3 marks: 1 mark for delta = F L / (A E); 1 mark for consistent units (for example mm, N and N/mm^2 with E = 200 000); 1 mark for the value 1.77 mm.
Follow-through: if the area in (a) is wrong but is used correctly in (b), award the marks of (b) in full for the student's own area.

## Question 4 (5 marks)
1 mark: the test described (specimen loaded gradually, load and extension measured, stress-strain plotted).
1 mark: the elastic region (straight line, Hooke's law, the specimen recovers when unloaded).
1 mark: the yield point (permanent plastic deformation begins).
1 mark: the ultimate tensile strength as the maximum stress, and necking.
1 mark: fracture, and what the curve says about ductility.
A bare list of keywords with no explanation earns at most 2 marks.

## Question 5 (5 marks)
1 mark: the cylinder is cut along a diameter and one half is considered in equilibrium.
1 mark: bursting force from the pressure on the projected area, p d L.
1 mark: resisting force from the hoop stress on two wall sections, 2 sigma t L.
1 mark: the two forces equated.
1 mark: the result sigma = p d / (2 t).

## Question 6 (5 marks)
2 marks: first reason (for example visible deformation before failure gives warning), 1 for the idea, 1 for explaining why it matters.
2 marks: second reason (for example absorbs energy through plastic deformation, or redistributes load), 1 for the idea, 1 for the explanation.
1 mark: a suitable example of a ductile material (mild steel, copper, aluminium) with or without a contrast to a brittle one.
