Q1. Stress is force divided by area (σ = F/A). Strain is the extension divided by the original length. Hooke's law says stress is proportional to strain up to the elastic limit, σ = Eε.

Q2. r = 10 mm, so A = π × 10² = 314.2 mm².
F = 50 kN = 50 000 N.
σ = 50 000 / 314.2 = 159.1 MPa.

Q3a. Area = π × 12² = 452.4 mm².

Q3b. δ = FL/(AE) = (20 000 × 2000) / (452.4 × 200 000) = 0.44 mm.

Q4. A specimen of mild steel is pulled in a machine until it breaks, and the load and extension are measured. The first part of the graph is a straight line, which is the elastic region and it follows Hooke's law. Then there is the yield point where the steel starts to stretch permanently. After that the stress reaches its maximum, the UTS, and the specimen necks and then breaks.

Q5. Take half of the cylinder. The force from the pressure is p × d × L. The force in the two walls is 2 × σ × t × L. Equating the forces, σ = pd/(2t).
