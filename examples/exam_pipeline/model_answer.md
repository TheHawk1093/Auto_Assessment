# Model answers

## Question 1
Stress is the internal resisting force per unit area of a cross-section, sigma = F/A, measured in pascals. Strain is the change in length per unit original length, epsilon = dL/L, and has no units. Stress is the cause (the loading) and strain is the resulting deformation. Hooke's law: within the limit of proportionality, stress is directly proportional to strain, sigma = E epsilon, where E is Young's modulus, a constant of the material.

## Question 2
Diameter 20 mm, so the area is A = pi d^2 / 4 = pi x 20^2 / 4 = 314.16 mm^2. The load is 50 kN = 50 000 N. Stress = F / A = 50 000 / 314.16 = 159.15 N/mm^2 = 159.15 MPa.

## Question 3
(a) A = pi d^2 / 4 = pi x 12^2 / 4 = 113.1 mm^2.
(b) Elongation delta = F L / (A E) = (20 000 N x 2000 mm) / (113.1 mm^2 x 200 000 N/mm^2) = 1.77 mm. (Equivalently 1.77 x 10^-3 m.) If a student's area in (a) is wrong but the method in (b) is right for that area, (b) is credited in full.

## Question 4
A standard specimen of mild steel is gripped in a testing machine and loaded gradually while the load and the extension are recorded; stress and strain are plotted. Up to the limit of proportionality the graph is a straight line obeying Hooke's law, and the deformation is elastic: the specimen returns to its original length when unloaded. At the yield point the steel begins to deform plastically, strain grows with little increase in stress, and the deformation is permanent. After strain hardening the curve reaches the ultimate tensile strength, the maximum stress; the cross-section then reduces locally (necking) and the specimen fractures. The strain at fracture shows how ductile the material is.

## Question 5
Take a thin-walled cylinder of internal diameter d, length L and thickness t under internal pressure p, and cut it along a diameter. Consider one half in equilibrium. The pressure acts on the projected area d x L, so the bursting force is p d L. It is resisted by the hoop stress sigma acting on the two cut wall sections, each of area t L, giving a resisting force 2 sigma t L. For equilibrium 2 sigma t L = p d L, so sigma = p d / (2 t).

## Question 6
Ductile materials such as mild steel deform visibly before they fail, so an overload gives a warning and the structure can be evacuated or repaired. They also absorb a large amount of energy through plastic deformation and can redistribute load around stress concentrations, whereas a brittle material such as cast iron or glass fractures suddenly with little warning.
